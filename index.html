tiny-tensor-core · Verilog · ECP5

One digit, 273 clocks.

My 6,634-parameter MNIST network as a chip. Scroll, and the figure runs the real int8 weights through the same schedule as the Verilog, clock by clock.

  1. 01

    Layer 1 · 197 clocks

    One pixel per clock is broadcast to 32 multiply-accumulate lanes, each holding one neuron's weights. 6,272 MACs, 32 at a time.

  2. 02

    Requantize · 32 clocks

    No floats. Each accumulator becomes a 0–127 activation with one fixed-point multiply and a shift: (acc · 31,942 + B + 2²³) ≫ 24. The clamp at 0 is the ReLU.

  3. 03

    Layer 2 · 33 clocks

    The 32 activations stream into 10 accumulators, one per digit.

  4. 04

    Argmax · 11 clocks

    Ten compares, and out comes the digit. The Arduino UNO needed about 491,000 clocks for the same network; the predictions match on all 10,000 test images.

int8-rollout-engine · on the board · Oct 14, 2026

The simulator said yes. The chip said no.

The 8-lane planner went onto a Tang Nano 20K. It ran the right sequence, with the clock count exact every time, and got 0 of 20 answers right.

A/B builds narrowed it down. Turning off the carry chains changed nothing. Building the multipliers from LUTs instead of the chip's DSP blocks made it bit-exact. RTL simulation and the timing report never showed the fault. Checking the chip's answers did.

Then a logic analyzer on the busy pin: 186.000 µs per decision, which is 5,022 clocks at 27 MHz, exactly the RTL's count.
